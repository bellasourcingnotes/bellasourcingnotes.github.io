Supplier Verification

Finding a Factory Is Easy. Choosing the Right One Is Hard.

By Bella Cheng·7 min read·Shenzhen, China

Online platforms make supplier discovery fast. They do not make supplier selection automatic. The important question is not whether a factory exists. It is whether this supplier can reliably execute this requirement under the commercial conditions of your project.

Start with identity—but do not stop there

Confirm the registered company name, operating address, contact identity and payment beneficiary. Compare the names across quotations, invoices, certificates and bank details. Inconsistency needs an explanation before money moves.

This reduces basic counterparty risk, but registration alone says little about technical fit.

Ask for evidence tied to your product

Generic factory photographs and broad product catalogues are weak signals. Ask for evidence that connects directly to your process or critical requirement:

  • Relevant production equipment and how it is used
  • Similar materials, tolerances, finishes or assembly steps
  • Sample results and how problems were corrected
  • Inspection tools, test methods and record formats
  • Who owns technical communication during production

Pay attention to the questions they ask

A capable supplier usually asks useful questions. It may challenge an unrealistic tolerance, clarify the application, explain why a material behaves differently or propose a more practical sequence for samples and tooling.

Warning sign

Immediate agreement with every request can feel efficient. Sometimes it means the supplier has not examined the difficult details yet.

Use a sample as a test of the working relationship

A sample is not only a product check. It reveals communication speed, interpretation, documentation, packaging discipline and how the supplier responds when something is wrong. Record deviations and see whether the corrective action is specific and verifiable.

Match the verification depth to the risk

A low-value standard item and a custom component for specialized equipment should not follow the same process. Increase verification when tooling, safety, compliance, tight tolerance, long lead time or difficult after-sales support is involved.

The right supplier is not always the largest, cheapest or most polished. It is the one whose capability, communication and commercial behavior match the real demands of the order.

This article provides general sourcing information. Supplier verification should be adapted to the product, jurisdiction, payment structure and risk level.
