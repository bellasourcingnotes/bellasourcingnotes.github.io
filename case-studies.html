Selected project work

What local execution looks like in practice.

Five anonymized examples of projects where the challenge was not simply finding a supplier. It was translating, checking, documenting and following through.

Medical Device PrototypeItaly

From an ultrasound concept to prototype housings in 20 days—without production tooling.

An Italian client needed high-precision tabletop ultrasound housings for early demonstrations, but conventional injection molds would have required too much cost and time. I translated the early requirements into production-ready language, coordinated material, structure, finish and tolerance details with a Shenzhen prototype factory, and followed the work daily.

OutcomePrototype-ready housings in a 20-day production window, with reported cost savings of more than 50% by avoiding injection tooling at this stage.
Supplier VerificationUnited States

Validating a jewelry supplier and moving from payment to shipment in one week.

An early-stage founder had found a supplier online but needed confidence that the business was real and the samples would match expectations. I checked the supplier's background and capability, coordinated payment and packaging, and reviewed the samples before shipment.

OutcomeSamples were received locally the day after payment and shipped within one week, allowing the founder to begin market testing sooner.
Technical TranslationPakistan

Turning a photograph and rough measurements into manufacturable circuit-breaker files.

An experienced engineer had a product vision but no formal drawings. I translated handwritten notes and rough dimensions into a structure factories could work with, clarified alloy, function and construction details, and coordinated two rounds of samples.

OutcomeA clear production file and successful sample result—without the client traveling to China.
SKD DocumentationIndia

Breaking three equipment models into clear SKD documentation in one working day.

After two supplier visits, an Indian team still lacked the detailed structure required to move forward under new regulations. I created part-numbered lists with inside-to-outside photographs, recorded step-by-step assembly videos and organized the files for the client's team.

OutcomeThree models documented in 24 hours, helping the team avoid additional coordination trips and move forward with a shared visual reference.
Used EquipmentRisk Review

Separating a credible used laser machine from a listing full of warning signs.

A client needed second-hand laser processing equipment. I contacted the original manufacturer for production year, service history, documentation and reference pricing, then expanded the market search. One unusually cheap option lacked a nameplate, serial number, test video and seller transaction history.

OutcomeThe client gained a realistic market benchmark, avoided an unsafe listing and could evaluate price, documentation and maintenance risk with more context.

Client identities, supplier names and commercially sensitive details are withheld. Results reflect the scope and circumstances of each individual project and do not guarantee identical outcomes.
