Supplier audits in China before placing an order

Approved samples, unverified production

An Indian buyer's capacitor samples had passed testing. Before a bulk order, the buyer needed to know who would manufacture the parts and how quality and traceability would be controlled. The buyer joined my site visit by video. I reviewed the available quality documents, discussed labeling and batch records, and recorded which areas and processes were outside the scope of the visit.

The visit did not independently establish the relationship between the seller and the stated factory or verify production and shipment controls. I recommended obtaining written evidence of that relationship, starting with a small trial order and following up during normal production.

Early-stage supplier selection for a custom display

An Indonesian manufacturer needed to assess whether a supplier could develop and reliably supply a specific 32-inch display. During the visit, the buyer joined remotely. I reviewed the facilities and asked about production ownership, test records, capacity, warranty and spare-parts support. The target model was absent and the lines were idle, so I recorded the supplier's capacity figures as unverified.

The buyer received a list of evidence to request before committing, including recent production, inspection and aging-test records for the target model, and a follow-up visit while it was in production.

Before booking an audit: Tell me what decision the visit needs to support. An approved sample calls for checking who will make the order and how batches will be traced. A product still in development calls for evidence of model-specific capability, testing and long-term support. I report what I saw, what the supplier reported and what still needs a follow-up.

Anonymized pre-order audit summary

The buyer's capacitor samples had passed testing. Before placing a bulk order, the buyer needed to know who would make the parts and how the production batches would be controlled.

What I observed
I reviewed the quality documents made available during the visit, including a final inspection report. Access to the production areas was restricted, and the warehouse and dispatch areas were outside the visit scope.
What the supplier reported
The seller said it handled international sales and that a separate manufacturing entity would produce the parts.
What still needed verification
The seller's relationship with the stated manufacturer, the actual production and shipment controls, and how batch identifiers would link to inspection and shipment records.
Recommended next step
Ask for written evidence of the manufacturing relationship and agree on what must be recorded for each batch. Use a small trial order to check quality, delivery and consistency of records before scaling. Plan a follow-up visit during normal production if longer-term cooperation is likely.

Market entry and product certification

Entering China with a medical device

A Korean bone densitometry manufacturer needed to understand China's market before choosing a product position or channel partner. I compared procurement patterns and competing models, compiled domestic and imported product registrations, and mapped the companies behind selected brands. The resulting report gave the client a basis for discussing product positioning and screening potential partners.

Sourcing a custom adapter for India

An Indian buyer needed a power adapter built to its specifications. I evaluated four suppliers, discussed development and testing with manufacturers, and checked whether existing BIS certification documents covered the proposed configuration. I summarized the open questions about the exact model, production site, minimum order size, testing costs and who would handle certification.

The buyer could compare a custom build in China with a supplier offering production in India, and identify what to confirm before commissioning samples or committing to a purchase.

Before committing to a market or supplier: If you're entering China, start with registered models, the companies behind them and who buys them. If you're sourcing from China for another market, ask the supplier's technical or regulatory team to confirm in writing whether the certificate covers the proposed model, factory and, where applicable, the importer. Agree who will handle any additional testing and what it will cost.

Small-batch sourcing and order follow-up in China

These projects show how I help buyers keep track of parts, suppliers, payments and shipments when several steps need someone on the ground.

  • Replacing an obsolete circuit breaker. Using a Pakistani buyer's drawings and specifications, I coordinated the custom replacement through more than ten rounds of testing. The replacement was delivered and the existing equipment returned to operation.
  • Managing recurring multi-supplier orders. For a buyer in Türkiye, a typical month involved over 80 payments, communication with over 50 suppliers, checks on more than 100 parcels and over 20 dispatches. I sent a reconciliation every three days and a parcel inspection list with photos and video every two days, so the buyer could match payments, orders and receipts.
  • Combining small R&D orders. An Indian medical equipment team needed small quantities of PCBs, connectors, displays and custom metal frames. I coordinated supplier communication, sample payments, receipts, checks and combined shipments, giving the buyer one point of contact.
  • Shortlisting solar module suppliers. For an Indonesian project, I shortlisted three factories for volume orders, custom sizes and trial batches. I found no evidence of Indonesian SNI certification or local production for the three candidates at that stage. Support for SNI certification, a route to meeting TKDN local-content requirements and customs clearance eligibility still needed confirmation.
  • Preparing kits for local assembly. I documented three models for an Indian team in one working day, with numbered component lists, photographs and step-by-step assembly videos for semi-knocked-down (SKD) assembly.
  • Checking a used laser machine before payment. I established a baseline with the original manufacturer. A low-price listing lacked identification, test footage and a verifiable seller history. The buyer ruled out that listing.

Before handing over a project: Share the drawings or specifications, quantities, supplier list and your next decision. Agree on the proof you need at each step, such as parcel photos, test records, a payment reconciliation or an assembly guide.

Medical Device PrototypeItaly

From an ultrasound concept to prototype housings in 20 days—without production tooling.

An Italian client needed high-precision tabletop ultrasound housings for early demonstrations, but conventional injection molds would have required too much cost and time. I translated the early requirements into production-ready language, coordinated material, structure, finish and tolerance details with a Shenzhen prototype factory, and followed the work daily.

OutcomePrototype-ready housings in a 20-day production window, with reported cost savings of more than 50% by avoiding injection tooling at this stage.
